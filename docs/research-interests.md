# Research interests and selection policy

The user prioritizes AI, machine learning, multimodal learning and intelligent autonomous systems, with applications to autonomous driving and human-centered mobility. Broader multimodal AI, LLM/VLM/VLA, reasoning, grounding and uncertainty methods are relevant when their transfer to real-world perception, prediction, interaction or decision-making is testable.

Priority areas:

1. Multimodal learning across visual, textual, temporal and behavioral information.
2. Driving behavior/intention, trajectory prediction, driving styles and traffic interactions.
3. Explanation faithfulness, grounding, uncertainty, interpretable and reliable decisions.
4. Driver state/emotion, human-AI interaction and adaptive in-vehicle assistance.
5. Foundation models for intelligent systems beyond driving.

Select evidence-backed methods, benchmarks, datasets and reproducible findings. Paper popularity or recency is not a quality guarantee. Novelty is tentative until full-text and broader prior-work checks are performed. Start with low-cost, falsifiable experiments, ideally CPU-feasible pilots; distinguish resource assumptions from measured runtimes. Do not infer driver emotion ground truth from facial appearance alone. Proposed experiments and publication require explicit human approval.

Candidate projects include WOMD-Reasoning and an emotion-aware in-vehicle assistant. First establish dataset access, license, supervision and evaluation protocols. WOMD motion tracks are not automatically a paired image-text driving-explanation dataset; extra sensor modalities and explanatory labels require separate verification.
